# The Fleet

The fleet is a Claude Code or Codex session that plans a mission with you, then babysits it (*Fleet agent…* in the settings menu: its Manager and its team run on the same CLI). Link one to a group from the group menu (*Link a fleet*) and describe the goal in its terminal. Then:

1. **It proposes a plan.** Once you have answered its questions and finished reviewing, it splits the goal into sub-tasks, each with an owner, a time budget and, optionally, a difficulty level, and restates in the plan what you asked, so you can check one against the other. The plan appears in the mission panel under the fleet. A sub-task may wait for named tasks of the mission (`waits_for`: titles, nothing else): it starts from the queue, for a new agent, once each of them is done and merged, so later waves can be planned before the first is finished.
2. **You launch the mission.** dummyPlane creates the mission branch `dummyplane/<mission>`, plus a branch and a worktree for each agent next to your repository. A worktree starts bare, with no dependencies installed: the brief of an agent that starts in a new one says so, with the install command read from the project's lockfiles (`pnpm-lock.yaml`, `package-lock.json`, `yarn.lock`, `bun.lock`, `uv.lock`, `poetry.lock`, `Gemfile.lock`, `composer.lock`; a Cargo project needs none), or points to the project's note on its setup when it has one (a note whose name has `setup`, `install`, `onboarding`, `getting-started` or `bootstrap`). Nothing is installed for it. Then dummyPlane itself, not the fleet's model, sets the mission up: a **Kanban board** right of the fleet, one card per sub-task in its to-do column, and a **Manager** terminal under the board, in a group of its own named *Mission <name>*. The new agents open in that group as the Manager's sub-agents, in a tree that grows downward under it, four to a row, the group growing with it; their own sub-agents join it too. The board stays outside the group: it is the record of the work, and outlives the Manager, whose group goes with it at the clean-up once the team has left. A mission planned from a Kanban column (*Plan with the fleet*) runs on that board instead of a new one.
3. **The Manager runs the board.** It gives every agent its sub-task, reads their reports, merges, reassigns and finishes. The cards follow: *In progress* when an agent takes one, *Reported, not merged* on its "done" report, *Done* once its branch is merged, *Blocked* when it is blocked or failed or its agent stopped before reporting. A waiting task whose card stands in *Done* leaves the queue. Each agent declares its steps, which appear as a timeline under its terminal, and agents can hand parts of their work to their own sub-agents (a step an agent hands over, named in `spawn_agent`'s `step`, goes in progress when the sub-agent opens and follows its report). With *Sub-agents as terminals* on, the fleet's `spawn_agent` opens a terminal beside it for a one-off task instead. While its mission runs, that one-off sub-agent, and every agent opened under it in turn, takes level low or medium only (an Opus or Fable named by hand counts as high): a high or extreme task goes into the mission with `add_tasks`, or the fleet asks you first, and an agent below it tells its parent. The mission's own agents, their Manager and what they open are not held to it, and the limit lifts when the mission ends. Those one-off agents are on the mission's board too, so you see everything the fleet started: each gets a card in *In progress* when it opens, titled by its task and marked *Ad hoc, outside the plan* in its notes, which follows its report (*Done* on "done", *Blocked* with an alert on "blocked" or "failed"; there is no branch to merge). Only the fleet's own one-off agents and the ones opened under them get a card, while its mission runs, and only on the board the mission runs on. `team` shows each agent as its pill reads it (working, waiting, idle, done, blocked, failed, stopped) with how long it has been so; "blocked" means an agent needs a decision or help from outside, failing checks are not blocked. dummyPlane keeps each sub-task with the mission until it is typed: if dummyPlane restarts first, or the agent's terminal stops before its session opens, the sub-task goes once the terminal runs again. When an agent stops before its work is done, the Manager can `relaunch` it on its own conversation and tell it what is left, or `reassign` its sub-task to a new agent that opens in the stopped agent's worktree, on its branch, with its commits and uncommitted changes, its report and its steps. An agent that finished without calling `report` (idle at its prompt, commits on its branch) is recorded as done by the Manager's `merge` with `agent` set: its seat frees, its card follows, the queue goes on. `merge` never skips an agent silently: it names every one it leaves out and why (not reported, at work, fixing its conflict), and one whose worktree holds uncommitted changes is merged without them, with the advice to ask it to commit and merge again. The Manager can close the terminal of a team agent that has reported or stopped (`close_agent`), never one at work, waiting, on a dialog or still starting; its report, card, branch and worktree stay. If dummyPlane restarts while a mission runs, the mission comes back with its fleet or its Manager: once you resume either one, dummyPlane resumes the Manager and every agent still on its sub-task on its own conversation, draws the mission's wires again, and tells the Manager which agents are back and which are not, and why. If the Manager's terminal is closed (or stops) while the mission runs, even before a restart, the fleet can bring it back with `relaunch`: it reopens in its team's group under the board (the mission keeps that group, so it is found again even when every agent that stood in it was closed, and no second group is made), on its own conversation, the team stopped meanwhile starts again, and the Manager hears what it runs and where the team stands. The fleet does so only when the mission must go on or you ask, and tells you in one line that it did and why, since you may have closed it on purpose; close it again and it asks you instead. `stop_mission` stays the way to end a mission; once the mission is over, `relaunch` is refused and the fleet plans again.
4. **You keep talking to the fleet.** The team never interrupts it: reports, questions and progress go to the Manager and the board. Ask for more and the fleet adds cards, which the Manager's queue starts as soon as a seat frees up; the fleet can also reorder them, look at the board and the team, or stop the mission. **A plan that turns out wrong is amended, not restarted.** Once the mission runs, `propose_plan` proposes an *amendment* to the tasks still waiting in the queue: tasks to add, to replace (under the same title) or to drop. It appears in the mission panel as the first plan did, with what it adds, edits and drops, and the status line asks for you; nothing changes until you press *Apply the changes* (*Decline* tells the fleet and changes nothing). Tasks an agent holds, has reported or has finished are never touched: an amendment naming one is refused, by name. If a task started while you were reading, the changes are applied whole or not at all: you are told, and the fleet proposes them again. Applied, the board follows (a new task gets a card, a dropped task's card goes if the board was made for the mission, and stays on a board you handed over) and the Manager and the fleet are told. An overnight run's amendment waits for you like any other. Besides your answer to an amendment, it hears two things only: the board is finished (with its summary), or the Manager is stuck and needs you.
5. **The Manager keeps time.** It is told when a sub-task has used 80 % of its budget, and again when it is used up (not on an overnight run, whose budgets are only targets). At twice the budget you get a notification and a red bar. Nobody is stopped automatically: the Manager can extend, interrupt or reassign. Time when the whole mission only waits for you (an agent held by a dialog or an expired login, nobody else at work) does not count against the budgets. **Stalls do not go unnoticed:** dummyPlane looks at the team every 15 seconds and tells whoever can act, once per stall: the Manager (or the Fleet, when it runs the mission itself) hears that a team agent has been idle for 10 minutes without reporting (read_output first: a command may still run in the background), or has waited 10 minutes on a dialog; the Fleet hears that its Manager has been idle for 5 minutes while a report waits for a decision, or while the team's work is done and its reports wait to be merged (a Manager at rest while a teammate still works is left alone). An agent idle at an expired login is told to you after 3 minutes, and a dialog nobody answered for 10 minutes again: a popup, an entry in the notification panel and, with the window away, a system notification. A sub-agent's report typed into a parent that never took it up (its terminal compacted while idle) is typed again after 3 minutes.
6. **It merges.** Each finished agent's work is squashed into the mission branch as one commit, named after its sub-task, with what the agent reported (and what it left to check by hand) as the message's body; no branch names, and the agent's own commits stay out of the mission's history. Merged again later, an agent's branch brings only what it committed since. A conflict, or a commit git refuses, undoes that merge, leaves the mission's worktree as it was, and is reported. The agent then merges the mission's branch into its own and resolves the conflict there: nothing merges its branch again by itself meanwhile (the queue's automatic merge, or a `merge` of the others, would undo its resolution mid-way); it is merged again when it reports, or when the Manager's `merge` names it. A file every agent would touch (a CHANGELOG, release notes, a version number) is given to one last task that waits for the others, not to each: the role prompts say so, as parallel edits of one file conflict at every merge. Mission merges run none of the repository's hooks, as they are dummyPlane's own bookkeeping, and neither does a landing (below). **Agents clean up after themselves:** once an agent made for the mission is merged, with its report done and nothing left uncommitted, it closes with its own sub-agents, and once their processes have exited its worktree and branch are removed; then their terminals leave the canvas. Its report stays on its card and in the panel, and its next sub-task goes to a new agent. Git is never forced: a folder still held, uncommitted files or commits made after the merge stay where they are, and the Manager is told which and why. A linked agent and a blocked or failed one keep everything until *Clean up the mission*. The cleanup waits up to a minute for the turn the agent reported in to end, as the Manager merges at its report. One still at work after that minute, one handed a sub-task, one whose own sub-agents still work and one you are using stay open, and the Manager is told why once. Each later merge tries again without a word and closes it once it is free; otherwise *Clean up the mission* does. With *Stop finished agents* off in Settings, nothing is cleaned up before *Clean up the mission*, and a merged agent can still be handed another sub-task. A mission's own steps **never push**, and reach **your own branch** only when you click *Land* (below). The git window pushes only when you click *Push*, and an agent running commands in a terminal is limited only by its permissions.

**What the fleet shows, and what it asks of you.** It shows a result (a verdict, a table, a comparison) with `show_output`, in an output node beside it, rather than opening an agent to draw one. Before it asks you for anything it does what its own tools can do: it points an agent at a file only after it has seen the file exist (otherwise the content goes in the brief), and what only you can do (`/login`, `/mcp`, a connector or an MCP server to add or approve) it says once, with the reason, then goes on with the work that does not need it. The Manager asks the fleet, not you, for a file or a fact the fleet pointed it to. Neither offers to save a session handoff or a memory unless you ask for one or its context is really near its limit.

**What a cleanup does with your agents' ignored files.** A worktree goes with its ignored files, and git does not look at those, so dummyPlane does. What a build or a tool makes again is deleted with the worktree: the folders `node_modules`, `target`, `dist`, `build`, `out`, `.next`, `.nuxt`, `.output`, `.svelte-kit`, `.turbo`, `.swc`, `.vite`, `.parcel-cache`, `.angular`, `.astro`, `.docusaurus`, `.expo`, `.pnpm-store`, `.cache`, `coverage`, `.nyc_output`, `__pycache__`, `.pytest_cache`, `.mypy_cache`, `.ruff_cache`, `.tox`, `.venv`, `venv`, `.gradle` and `obj`, at any depth, and the files `.DS_Store`, `Thumbs.db`, `desktop.ini`, `*.pyc`, `*.tsbuildinfo`, `.eslintcache`, `.stylelintcache` and `next-env.d.ts`; so is an ignored file that is an untouched copy of the same file in your own checkout. Any other ignored file (an `.env.local`, notes, fonts a script copied, a `bin` or `vendor` folder, a log) is never deleted:
- *Clean up the mission* and *Clean up leftovers* **move** it, before the worktree goes, to a folder beside the worktrees, `<project>.worktrees\.kept\<worktree>\`, at the same place inside (`.kept\web-hulk\public\fonts\…`). Every `.env*` file goes there too, ignored or not: no commit ever takes one. The notice and the fleet name the folder. Nothing there is overwritten: a second clean-up of a worktree of the same name moves into `.kept\<worktree>-2`. Delete the folder yourself once you have looked.
- An agent's own cleanup after its merge, and the undo of a launch that failed, go by the same rules, with the same quick read: what the agent left uncommitted is committed on its own branch (never an `.env*`), what nothing makes again of its ignored files moves to the same `.kept` folder, the worktree's status is read again, and only then is its folder taken away. The branch of a worktree that had uncommitted work is kept (`auth-hulk (WIP saved at clean-up)`), and the Manager is told in one line what was saved and where the files went; a folder a program holds, commits no branch holds, or a merge under way keep the worktree, never forced.

Fleet, board, Manager and team are joined by wires, and *Arrange* moves them as one block. Between two Arranges they stay together too: dragging the fleet takes its mission along, dragging the board takes the Manager and team, and a board left right of the fleet stays under the fleet's own sub-agents, moving down or back up as they come and go, while the sub-agents stay beside the fleet. A board you moved elsewhere stays where you put it. When one agent messages another through dummyPlane (a `message_agent`, the Manager's `write`, a report, a card handed from the fleet to the board to the Manager, a message between the fleet and its Manager, `message_manager` one way and `message_fleet` the other, through the board), a dashed wire forms between them if none was there, and lights up with every message. A mission's agents and their Manager reach each other without a manual link. The mission panel says who runs the mission, with the Manager and the board a click away. Only the mission's end alerts you, once: neither the Manager nor its team do.

**A manager outside a mission.** Any agent can also open a lighter manager of its own: `spawn_agent` with `role: "manager"` opens an ordinary sub-agent (the same CLI, folder, account, permissions, model routing and pick of skills, MCP servers and plugins as any other) whose task comes after a short brief. The brief has it run the task instead of doing it: it opens a board of its own with `open_board`, one card per item, opens one sub-agent per card, as many at once as *Sub-agents at once* allows, moves the cards as their reports come in, asks the agent that opened it with `message_agent` when a decision is not its own, and finishes with `report`. It keeps every tool an agent has: the brief, not a restriction, makes it a manager. It is no mission's Manager: it has no queue, merges nothing and makes no worktree, and its header and its row in the Agents panel say *Manager*, where a mission's Manager's title says *Manager · mission*. It counts as one level and one seat like any sub-agent, so it is refused where its own sub-agents would be deeper than the limit, and a manager cannot open another. A Fleet's `spawn_agent` does not offer it: a Fleet splits its work through its mission.

With the **model router**, each sub-task's level picks the model its agent runs on:

| Level | Model |
|---|---|
| `low` | Claude Haiku 5.5 |
| `medium` | Claude Sonnet 5.5 |
| `high` | Claude Opus 5.5 |
| `extreme` | Claude Fable 5.2 |

**Seats.** Two limits cap the agents at work, and a refusal names which one: the number of sub-agents running at once in Settings, counted over every project, and a mission's own *Max active agents*. A terminal you close, an agent that exits to its shell (for the sub-agents' limit) and an agent that reported frees its seat at once. A refused fleet or Manager is told to wait for a report that frees a seat, or to queue the task with `add_tasks`; only an agent with a shell may be told to do the work itself. Giving new work to a sub-agent that already reported (`message_agent`) takes a seat again, so while the sub-agents' limit is full that message is refused in the same words; a message to an agent still at work, or one that only answers, always goes through.

The router also picks the model of every sub-agent opened with `spawn_agent` under it: the sub-agents of a fleet whose router is on, of an agent on that fleet's running mission, of a terminal whose own router is on, and the sub-agents those open in turn. The switches are read each time a sub-agent opens, so turning one off stops it for everything below. A sub-agent's own switch stays off while a router above it routes what it opens.

Under a router, a sub-agent takes the model of the `level` it is given, and `medium` when it is given none. A model named beside a level is set aside, and a call that names a model with no level is refused, with the levels to choose from: an agent used to asking for one model would otherwise keep every sub-agent on it. To run a sub-agent on a model of your own choosing, turn the router off or set the model in the terminal's Edit panel (the pencil in its header).

**Costly levels are for thinking.** An agent's `spawn_agent` also takes an optional `kind`, what the work is: `write` (writing files from a settled plan, mechanical edits, boilerplate) and `verify` (running tests and reading them, lint and format, search sweeps, a verification pass) are routine, and open at `medium` at most: a `high` or `extreme` level given with them is lowered to `medium`, and the answer says so. `plan`, or no kind, keeps the level given. The lowered level is the one everything reads, so routine work an ad-hoc agent of a running mission rates `high` opens at `medium` instead of being refused. A sub-agent the router opens at `high` or `extreme` is told, before its task, which model it runs on and that it is kept for planning, design, hard debugging and the final review: it hands the routine work to sub-agents of its own at `low` or `medium`, one per separable chunk with its plan in the task, does a small edit already in its context itself, and does the work itself when no seat is free. That note is left out for a manager (its own brief hands everything down) and for a sub-agent at the depth limit, which can open none. Every agent's instructions carry the same rule: rate the part handed over, not the project it belongs to. The kind is the spawner's own word: dummyPlane holds routine work declared as such to `medium`, but cannot tell that an agent on a costly model types the files itself. A Fleet's `spawn_agent` has no `kind`: its one-off sub-agents are lookups and checks, held to `low` or `medium` while its mission runs.

Every terminal shows the switch, in its header, its ⋯ menu and its Edit panel, but it acts only on Claude and Codex, the CLIs that get dummyPlane's tools and so open sub-agents. On any other CLI or a shell it is greyed out, off, and says why when you point at it: nothing opens sub-agents there. So is a Manager's: it opens none, and its fleet's switch is the one that routes the mission.

The fleet and the Manager talk to dummyPlane through a local MCP server. The fleet plans with `propose_plan` and babysits with `add_tasks`, `prioritize`, `board`, `team`, `message_manager`, `relaunch` (its Manager), `stop_mission`, `land`, `clean_up_mission`, `clean_up_leftovers` and `remote_control`; the Manager runs the team with `team`, `read_output`, `write`, `extend`, `interrupt`, `relaunch`, `reassign`, `merge`, `land`, `close_agent`, `move_card`, `finish`, `need_user` and `message_fleet`. `message_manager` types one line into the Manager's terminal (what you want the team to hear, a correction, an answer), and the Manager answers with `message_fleet`; while no Manager runs the mission (before the go-ahead, once it is finished, a Manager stopped or closed, a mission run the old way), the fleet is told why and nothing is sent. Both can read the repository, but they are denied `Edit`, `Write`, `NotebookEdit` and `Bash`: the agents write the code. A Codex fleet and its Manager run in Codex's read-only sandbox with approvals off, and get none of your own MCP servers, plugins or browser tools; `remote_control` is Claude's only. A mission launched before the Manager existed is run to its end by its fleet, the old way. A call with a wrong argument is refused with what the tool takes, so the next one can go right: `reassign` names the sub-task by its title, the agent holding it, its card's id or the start of its title (several that fit are listed, none is picked by guess); `write` takes `text`; an agent that is not on the team is refused with the team's names; a title over 60 characters is refused with its length and a cut one that would do; a title already held is refused with who holds it.

The mission's reads stay short however big the mission is, since Claude Code sets an MCP answer of more than 25,000 tokens aside in a file and the agent then reads that file instead (the case with `mission`, `team` and `board` on a big mission). `team` gives one line per agent (state, how far through its steps, time, its report cut short), `board` and `linked_boards` one line per card (notes cut short, the fields at their default left out), `mission` and `queue` each brief cut short; a text cut ends in `… (N more characters: <the call that reads it whole>)`, or `… (+N)` for a card's notes, the board's own note then naming the call. Each reads one item whole when asked by its name: `team` with `agent`, `mission` with `subtask`, `queue` with `task`, `board` and `linked_boards` with `card` (an id or a title). A list too long for one answer ends with how many are left out and the call that reads them. `merge` says what it merged and the conflicts, and counts the agents already merged with nothing new instead of naming them again. It reads every agent already merged at once, and the clean-up's check of the worktrees does too: a call took 2 to 5.7 s when git read them one after another.

## Landing a mission

The mission's work reaches your branch only when you click **Land**. Once the work is merged and checked, the Manager calls `land` (so does the fleet when you ask it, or once the mission is finished): the mission panel under the fleet, and a toast, ask *Land the mission on main?* and list the commits your branch would get. `land` only asks. It never lands, whatever an agent says you agreed to: only your click in dummyPlane does, and the agent that asked hears how it ended. The toast goes by itself when the question no longer stands (the mission was cleaned up, or its fleet was closed or deleted; a mission that only ended can still be landed, so its question stays), and a new `land` that finds nothing left to land takes the old question away and tells the agent that asked it.

- **Land fast-forwards your branch**, the one your checkout was on at the go-ahead, to the commit you were shown, in the folder where that branch is checked out; checked out nowhere, only the branch moves. Nothing is pushed, no pull request is opened, no remote is called, and your repository's hooks do not run.
- **Uncommitted changes refuse it.** With changes in tracked files of your checkout, nothing is touched (no stash, no reset): you and the agent that asked are told which files. Untracked files stay where they are, and git itself refuses a landing that would overwrite one.
- **If your branch moved since the go-ahead,** Land merges it into the mission's branch, in the mission's worktree, never in your checkout, and lands nothing yet. The Manager hands a team agent a sub-task to run the checks again there (once the mission is finished, the fleet opens an agent for it), then asks you again. A conflict is undone and goes to an agent, which makes that merge again in the mission's worktree, resolves it and commits it there.
- **Not now** lands nothing, and the agent that asked is told.
- **Land before you clean up:** *Clean up the mission* (or the fleet's `clean_up_mission`) removes the mission's worktree. After a landing, the Manager finishes and the fleet cleans up.

## Cleaning up (closing) a mission

*Clean up the mission* in the mission panel, or the fleet's `clean_up_mission`, is how a finished mission is closed. It never loses work:

- **The team's terminals close** when they are done: reported, idle at their prompt, stopped or abandoned, with their own sub-agents; the Manager closes too. A terminal at work, waiting for an answer, held by a dialog, still starting or in use by you stays open, and so does the worktree it is in: the cleanup says which, and why.
- **Every worktree goes, in this order.** (1) Whatever an agent left uncommitted (a dead agent's work included) is committed on its own branch, as one commit named *WIP saved at clean-up*: new files too, never what `.gitignore` leaves out and never an `.env*` file, with your git identity, no hooks, no signing and no trailer; nothing is pushed. (2) Its ignored files nothing makes again, and its `.env*` files, move to `<project>.worktrees\.kept\<worktree>\` (above). (3) Its status is read again: a change or a file to keep that appeared meanwhile keeps the worktree. (4) Only then is its folder moved whole into `<project>.worktrees\.trash\` (a program holding a file in it refuses that, and the worktree stays as it was, never half deleted), git forgets it, and the folder is deleted: links (pnpm's junctions) are removed, never followed, and paths past Windows' 260 characters are reached. A worktree stays, named with why, when a merge or rebase is under way there, when its HEAD stands on commits no branch holds, when `git worktree lock` locked it, or when a terminal is open in it.
- **It is quick, and answers in time.** Each worktree's status is read once (git does not walk into `node_modules`), and the worktrees are read, saved, moved and deleted side by side. The fleet's `clean_up_mission` and `propose_plan` answer within about 20 seconds whatever git takes: a cleanup still running then goes on, the fleet is told it is under way, and its result comes to the fleet's terminal as a `[dummyPlane]` line, which waits while you have typed there without sending. A second call, or a click on the button meanwhile, starts nothing and says the cleanup is under way. `propose_plan` then takes no plan: the fleet sends it again once the result has come.
- **Branches already on your branch are deleted:** the mission's branch once it is landed, and each agent's branch whose work is in it (dummyPlane's own record of each squash says so, since a squashed branch is no ancestor). Every other branch is kept and listed with why (*not merged into the mission's branch*, *3 commits not on main*, *WIP saved at clean-up*). Only branches dummyPlane made for this mission are ever deleted: never your branch, nor one checked out somewhere.
- **You see what was kept.** A notice lists what was removed, the work saved, each branch, worktree and terminal kept and why; it stays until you dismiss it when anything was kept. The fleet is told the same.

The fleet's `clean_up_mission` refuses while the mission runs, after an overnight run (its report is yours), while its Manager or an agent made for it is at work, and while a card of the mission is not done or a task still waits. Uncommitted work and unmerged branches no longer refuse it: the cleanup saves the one and keeps the other.

### Clean up leftovers

A cleanup frees the fleet for the next mission even when it kept a worktree or a branch: what it kept stays reachable through **Clean up leftovers**, a button in the mission panel between missions and on a finished one, or the fleet's `clean_up_leftovers` tool. It covers every `dummyplane/*` worktree and branch that past missions left in the project, whatever left them (a worktree kept at a cleanup, a mission freed before the fix, an app quit in the middle of a cleanup).

- **It shows a list before it acts.** Each worktree with what becomes of it: removed; its uncommitted work committed on its own branch first (*WIP saved at clean-up*); its ignored files and `.env*` moved to `<project>.worktrees\.kept\<worktree>\`; or kept, and why (a terminal is open in it, commits on no branch, a merge or rebase under way). Each branch: deleted when its work is already on your branch (an ancestor of the branch your checkout is on), else kept as *unlanded* with the count of commits not on it. Folders a cleanup left half deleted in `.trash` are deleted; folders beside the worktrees that git does not list are named and left alone.
- **Only your click acts.** *Clean up* reads everything again and does only what the list showed, by the same rules as *Clean up the mission*: a branch that moved since the list was shown is kept. *Not now* does nothing. The fleet's tool only raises the question; no agent can clean up by saying you agreed.
- **The question goes when it is moot.** Its toast and its prompt leave without a click when its fleet is closed or deleted, or when a new read finds nothing left (the agent that asked, if another one, is told). The outcome of a clean-up that kept something stays until you dismiss it, one toast per fleet: running it again replaces it.
- **When everything left is kept, it asks nothing.** What is kept, an unlanded branch or a worktree with a terminal open in it, stays kept every time until its reason goes. So when nothing would be removed or deleted, there is no *Clean up* that would change nothing. A notice names each kept item and why, and the fleet is told not to ask again before the reason goes (the branch landed on yours, the terminal closed).
- **What it never touches:** the worktrees and branches of a mission a fleet still holds (running, or finished and not yet cleaned up; the button refuses while a mission is starting), your own checkout, any branch that is not `dummyplane/*`, and any worktree outside `<project>.worktrees`. Nothing is pushed.
- With no record of a past mission's squashes, an agent's branch squashed into a mission you landed reads as unlanded and stays: delete it yourself once you have checked.

No agent of a mission works in your checkout: each has a worktree of its own, a merge-only mission's too, and the role prompts forbid planning a merge, stash or commit in your folder. That is a rule the agents follow, and Claude's agents are held to it by the guard: a `git` command that changes your checkout (`checkout`, `switch`, `merge`, `rebase`, `reset`, `stash`, `commit`, `pull`, `add`, `restore`, `clean`, `branch -D`, `worktree remove`, `tag -d`…), whether written `git -C <your folder> …`, `--work-tree`, `GIT_DIR=…`, or after a `cd`, `pushd` or `Set-Location` into it, asks you first (a refusal under `dontAsk`, telling the agent to run it in its own worktree). Reading it (`status`, `log`, `diff`, `show`, `rev-parse`, `branch --list`, `stash list`) goes on, and so does git in the agent's own worktree. dummyPlane's own *Land* never goes through an agent's shell, so it is not asked about. It is a text rule, like the delete guard, and it is for the commands that name your folder: a folder it cannot work out (`git -C "$(…)"`, a `cd` into a variable) is not read as yours and goes on, an agent that is not Claude is not covered, and a script on disk or an alias is not read (see *The push block is not a security boundary* below). It covers an agent started in one of dummyPlane's mission worktrees (`<project>.worktrees\<name>`); the fleet and the Manager have no shell at all, and the fleet's own one-off sub-agents work in your folder, where git is their job.

**Your folder lists the mission's branches while it runs.** git shares branches between a repository and its worktrees, so `git branch` (or your editor's branch list) in your folder shows `dummyplane/<mission>` and one branch per agent at work until you land the mission and clean it up; they cannot be hidden. An agent's branch goes as soon as its work is merged and it closes; after *Land* and the clean-up, only the branches that hold work not yet on yours are left, each named with why. Nothing of them moves your own branch: only your click on *Land* does. The fleet tells you this once, before you launch.

## Overnight run

Before you leave, open *Overnight run…* in the mission panel: how long you will be away, how many agents, where the tickets come from (GitHub issues with a label, `BACKLOG.md`, or Linear issues of one team with a label), what agents may do unasked and whether the plan waits for you. Until the team is formed, the night's choices stay in the panel, where *Edit…* changes them and *Cancel the night* calls it off. The fleet plans, and the Manager and its team work through a queue: when an agent reports done, its branch is merged into the mission branch and a new agent takes the next task in a new terminal, so each task starts with a fresh context. When you are back, a morning report in the panel gives what is done, what failed, what to check by hand and the time spent. Kanban is not a source yet. The PC stays awake during the mission, but closing the lid or asking for sleep still puts it to sleep.

Read this before you choose:

- **By default the plan waits for your go-ahead and agents may *Edit files*.** They edit files and run simple file commands (`mkdir`, `mv`, `cp`) unasked, and stop at their first other command until you are back: the Manager may answer such a prompt only *No*, since its *Yes once* is for missions in *Edit files and run commands*. That is the safer choice, and it limits what a night can do.
- ***Edit files and run commands* is an explicit choice.** Agents run any command unasked, in Bash or PowerShell alike, except `git push` and the `gh` commands that reach the remote, which are refused in both shells; what would still ask is refused: the network and the accounts logged in on this PC are open to them. Use a repository you can afford to lose.
- **A delete outside an agent's own folder asks you.** An `rm -rf` or `Remove-Item -Recurse` of something outside the folder the agent was started in (`D:\temp`, another worktree, your home) is held for your yes, through Claude's own permission prompt in its terminal, and a drive-relative path (`D:tempx`) or a drive's root is refused. The dialog names the target and whether it exists; the fleet, which cannot run commands, checks what it can before it tells you to approve, and says No when it could not check.
- **The same guard reads the other ways to delete.** `find … -delete` and `find … -exec rm`, `… | xargs rm -rf`, `cmd /c rd /s` and `del /s`, PowerShell's `Remove-Item -Recurse` under its aliases (`ri`, `rm`, `rmdir`, `del`, `erase`, `rd`) and from a `Get-ChildItem … | Remove-Item` pipeline, `[IO.Directory]::Delete(path, $true)`, and a script given to `bash -c`, `cmd /c`, `powershell -Command` or `-EncodedCommand`. Each is judged as `rm -rf` of what it reaches: it goes on inside the agent's own folder (and the system temp folder), asks you outside it (or for the whole folder, or its `.git`), and is refused for a drive's root or your home folder. `find . -name '*.pyc' -delete` and `rm -rf node_modules` pass unasked; `find . -delete` asks, as it empties the whole folder.
- **What the guard cannot place, it asks about.** It works a path out from the command itself: a variable the command set (`d=build; rm -rf $d`, `$d = Join-Path $PWD 'dist'`, `set X=out`), `$HOME`, `$TMPDIR`, `$env:TEMP`, `%USERPROFILE%`, `$PWD`, `mktemp -d`, a `for` loop over names in the folder, and a `cd`, `pushd` or `popd`. A recursive delete whose target it cannot work out (a variable nothing set, a command's output, `$_` of a pipeline, a folder lost by a `cd` into one) asks you, and the agent is told to print the variable and write the path out. A path that only ends in a variable and starts inside the agent's folder (`rm -rf dist/$pkg`) goes on, as it cannot leave it. In a mission or a night run, where nobody can answer, each of these asks is a refusal saying to delete specific files by name or ask the user. A command too long (over 256 KiB) or too tangled to be read in the hook's time asks too, and never goes on unread, nor does one the guard fails on. It is a text rule, not a sandbox: a script on disk (`bash run.sh`, `powershell -File`) and what a program deletes by itself (`git clean -fdx`, `rsync --delete`, `python -c 'shutil.rmtree(…)'`) are not read. A value that is all of what follows the agent's own folder or its `.git` (`rm -rf ./$x`, `rm -rf "$PWD/$x"`, `rm -rf .git/$x`, and the same with `${x}`, `$env:X`, `%X%` and `Remove-Item -Recurse`) asks like a path nothing set, since an empty value makes it the whole folder or its git history; only the end of a name (`dist/$pkg`, `build-$n`, `./$x.bak`) goes on.
- **Not every agent is covered.** The guard is a hook of Claude Code, so it reads Claude's commands only. Codex 0.167 has a `PreToolUse` hook, but it can only allow or refuse (its "ask" is not supported, and fails open), and a hook that dummyPlane's per-launch profile adds is skipped until you review and trust it with `/hooks`, which records the trust in your own Codex config; dummyPlane does not touch that file, nor does it start Codex with `--dangerously-bypass-hook-trust`, which would also run your other untrusted hooks. So a Codex agent in *Edit files* (`-s workspace-write -a on-request`) is held by Codex's sandbox: a delete outside its folder asks you or fails. A Codex agent in *Edit files and run commands* (`-s danger-full-access -a never`, which a commit needs) has only its prompt telling it not to delete outside its folder, and a Codex agent in *Ask* follows your own Codex config. The fleet and the Manager run in Codex's read-only sandbox. Gemini and the other terminals are started bare, with no role and no hook of dummyPlane's: nothing reads their deletes. The interface says so where you choose: the night dialog under *Edit files and run commands* for a Codex mission ("Recursive deletes outside the worktree are not blocked: only the agent's prompt says not to"), and the ＋ menu under Gemini ("Not guarded: recursive deletes outside the worktree are not blocked"); the other terminals are as unguarded, and say nothing yet.
- **The Manager answers some of its team's dialogs; the guard's Yes stays yours.** With `answer_dialog`, the Manager (and the fleet, for the Manager itself or a sub-agent it opened) may pick a permission request's *No*, or its *Yes* for this once only in a mission run in *Edit files and run commands* (in *Ask* or *Edit files* you approve the commands yourself), go on without a project's new MCP servers, or answer a question. It never picks *don't ask again*, *for this session* or a choice that changes the permission mode, and never logs in. A prompt the delete guard raised starts with `[dummyPlane-guard]`: the Manager may answer it only *No*, and its *Yes* is yours. Each answer shows a notice naming who answered what. `run_command` lets it run `/compact` or `/clear` in an idle agent of the team, and nothing else.
- **The fleet and its Manager never wait for a yes at night.** The fleet's session restarts on the same conversation in a mode that refuses whatever would ask, and the Manager starts in it; neither can edit files or run commands. *Clean up the mission* puts the fleet back as it was and closes the Manager.
- **Linear is read by the fleet, through Linear's own read-only server** (`https://mcp.linear.app/mcp/readonly`), with no key stored in dummyPlane. Choose *Linear*, then *Connect…*: the fleet restarts with Linear's server, and you sign in once in its terminal with `/mcp` (pick *linear*, then *Authenticate*); a Codex fleet opens Linear's sign-in in your browser instead (`codex mcp login`). dummyPlane cannot see that sign-in: if the fleet finds no Linear tool at night, it says so and plans nothing.
- **The push block is not a security boundary.** `git push` and the `gh` commands that reach the remote are denied by rules on the command's text and by a git setting in the terminal. They stop accidents. An agent running commands can get past them: a review did with `-c remote.origin.pushurl` and `GIT_CONFIG_COUNT=0`.
