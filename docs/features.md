# dummyPlane features

The full tour of what dummyPlane does. For the short version, see the [README](../README.md); for missions and the overnight run, see [The Fleet](fleet.md).

## Features

- **Real terminals on a canvas.** Every agent is a real PTY (ConPTY on Windows) drawn with xterm.js. You can pan, zoom, draw around the agents and annotate their work. The canvas is [Excalidraw](https://github.com/excalidraw/excalidraw), so shapes, arrows, text and images all work.
- **Closing a terminal stops everything it started.** Its agent's or shell's whole process tree ends with it, hidden helpers included: the commands of an agent's Bash tool, the dev servers it ran, its MCP servers. The same happens when its process ends by itself and when dummyPlane quits. On Windows, an app it opened that shows a window of its own, such as `code .` or a browser a login opened, stays open with its helpers: it is yours now. A console window is no such app, and a crash of dummyPlane closes those apps too. So a service an agent starts, Docker Desktop or a database for instance, stops with its terminal; the agents' instructions say so, and that one that must keep running is started outside the terminal's job, with `Invoke-CimMethod -ClassName Win32_Process -MethodName Create` in PowerShell, only when its task or you ask for it (the Fleet says so in the brief); otherwise the agent asks.
- **Teams.** Compose a group with a role (Frontend, Backend, Testers, Reviewers…). Each group takes its names from a pantheon (DC, Marvel, Throne of Glass, Star Wars or Lord of the Rings): Batman, Thor, Aelin…, picked when you compose the group or later from its menu. No agent takes the name of one that is still there in your project, on any of its canvases, nor of a member of a mission under way: once a pantheon is used up, the next agent takes a name of another one, and a number ("Batman 2") comes only when all five are in use.
  - **Add an agent** to a group from its ring (*Add agent*), the palette (*Add an agent…*) or its menu (the ⌄ of its label, the ⋯ of its header in the Agents panel). From the ring and the palette a list of the agents found on this machine opens at the pointer, the default agent ticked and under the keyboard: Up and Down, Enter or a click picks, Escape closes. The agent starts in the group's next free cell, in the folder of the workspace, as one undo; a folded group unfolds with it.
  - Give a group a **speciality** (one line, from its menu): a linked fleet reads it to hand each sub-task to the right group.
  - **Fold** a group onto its label with the chevron: its terminals leave the screen, nothing stops, and the label keeps its counters. Going to one of its agents unfolds it; a group can't be folded while its agents are still starting.
  - Save the team on screen as a **preset** (*Save the team as a preset…* in the function palette) and deploy it in any project with *Deploy team: <preset name>*. A preset keeps the shape only: groups, roles, specialities, agents and links, never instructions or history.
  - **Ungroup** from the selection ring, with `Ctrl+Shift+G` or the palette. *Remove from group* takes a single terminal or Fleet out of its group and leaves it where it is; a group left empty goes away.
  - **Link** two agents from an agent's foot: a wire joins them, each can message the other, and the two become a group, in one undo step. Two agents in no group get a new group where they stand (made around one of them, the other joining it, when something stands between them); an agent in no group joins the other's group, in its first free cell, with its outputs. Two groups are never merged: agents in different groups are linked, each stays in its own, and a notice says so. A notice also says why no group was made when its frame would cover a neighbour, when an agent would be parted from its sub-agents or its spawner, is a mission's Manager or would join a mission's group, or stands in a frame drawn by hand. Two agents already wired (a talk wire included) are left as they are.
- **Live states.** dummyPlane shows whether each agent is starting, ready, running, waiting for you, stopped or done, both on the canvas and in the Agents panel, which filters by All, Waiting, Working or Done; the title bar counts the agents working, and a click on it opens the panel on Working. For Claude Code the state comes from its hooks; for other agents, from their output. A minimised terminal stays on the canvas as a card that shows its state.
- **Back at the shell.** When Claude Code exits, its terminal stays open at the shell, reads *Agent exited* and offers to resume the conversation. Typing `claude` there yourself (`claude --resume`, `claude -c`…) starts it again as that terminal's agent, with its hooks, tools, role and limits, in PowerShell, pwsh, cmd and Git Bash.
- **One instruction box for everyone.** Address `@all` (everyone), a group, an agent, or several at once (`@batman/superman`). A line starting with `/` goes to the agents' CLI as a command, and dummyPlane suggests Claude Code's commands. The history keeps your last 50 instructions per project. An image pasted into the box shows as a thumbnail chip. With two agents or more and nothing targeted, the empty box suggests `Try "@all run the tests"` as its placeholder, until the first instruction is sent.
- **Agents talk to each other.** When one agent messages another, a dashed talk wire forms between them if none joins them, and it lights up with every message. Agents can also be linked by hand (see **Teams**). A message never lands in what you are typing: dummyPlane types it into a terminal only once the text you wrote there is sent (Enter) or cleared (Ctrl+C, Ctrl+U), or left alone for ten minutes; an agent's `write` to it is refused meanwhile, saying so. An agent you link to a Fleet ("+ Link", then a click on the Fleet) gets `hand_off` from its first session on, and the click tells you when the wire gives the agent less than you expect (a mission's team, a Fleet's own sub-agent).
- **Sub-agents with the skills, servers and plugins their task needs.** An agent that opens a sub-agent with `spawn_agent` chooses what it gets: `skills`, `mcp_servers` and `plugins` are all required, each a list of names among what the sub-agent's CLI (Claude Code or Codex) loads in that folder, which the agent reads with `installed_extras`. No kind is left to the CLI's defaults (every skill, plugin and server it loads): a call that leaves one out is refused and opens nothing, and an empty list gives none of that kind. A plugin brings its own skills and MCP servers, and dummyPlane's own server is always kept. dummyPlane checks every name before the terminal opens and again at each launch, so a restart or a resume starts the sub-agent with the same pick, and a sub-agent whose pick cannot be applied does not start. The pick shows under *Next launch* in the sub-agent's Edit panel, and its own sub-agents inherit it kind by kind unless they choose again. A pick may switch on what your settings leave off (a skill, a plugin with its servers, an MCP server), but never a project's MCP server you have not approved: only you let a repository's own server run, so no agent can enable one for it. A sub-agent gets at most what its spawner has: an agent restricted by its own spawner's pick, or by switches you turned off in its Edit panel, cannot give its sub-agents more (a name beyond is refused before the terminal opens and again at each launch), and a kind it leaves out stays as its own. An agent nothing restricts can give whatever its CLI lists; one that is restricted at all hands down only what it has, its pick or what is on by default, never an item your settings leave off. A Codex that is cold takes about a minute to list what it has installed: `spawn_agent` and `installed_extras` then say so within 20 seconds, with nothing opened, and the same call made again a moment later is answered at once. Spawns wait their turn one at a time: one that has waited past 25 seconds is refused too, so that none opens after its caller was told it did not. Limits: Claude Code's bundled skills are part of the CLI and stay, and a Claude sub-agent whose MCP servers were picked gets no claude.ai connector; on Codex, a plugin's or an app's MCP server follows its plugin, and a skill your Codex config turns off is not offered.
- **Output nodes.** An agent can show a result (a report, a table, a chart) in an output node beside its terminal, with the `show_output` tool. An agent in a group shows it inside the group, whose frame grows to hold it: it moves, arranges and folds with the group, leaves it with its agent, and stays on the canvas when the group is closed. A call without `output` updates the agent's latest output in place (a new one only when it has none on the canvas), `output: <id>` updates that one, and `new: true` shows another one beside it, so an agent that lost an output's id does not pile up new nodes. A `dummyplane://` link pasted into an agent's terminal is read with `read_node`, so the agent can open a terminal, a Fleet, a sub-agent, an output or a file from it. A Fleet has `show_output` too: its output opens beside the Fleet itself, wired to it (the wire is no team link), instead of the Fleet opening an agent to draw one.
- **A fleet that plans, a Manager that runs it.** Give the fleet a goal. It proposes a plan; at your go-ahead, a Kanban board and a Manager appear beside it. The Manager gives each agent a sub-task on its own git branch and worktree, watches the time budget and merges the results into a mission branch, while the fleet stays free to talk with you. Details are in [The Fleet](fleet.md).
- **Change review.** See what an agent changed, either uncommitted or since its base, browse the repository read-only, and click a line to leave a remark. All remarks go back to the agent in one message.
- **Hear about questions.** When an agent waits for you, a notice stays at the bottom left of the window until it stops waiting (this includes a question that keeps its session from opening, such as a project's new MCP servers or a login: dummyPlane reads it on the screen; the agent that opened it, or its mission's Manager, is told what it waits on, and its task is typed once you have answered), and a Windows notification appears if the window is not in focus (switch it off in the settings menu). A notification also tells you when an agent finishes (*Alert when an agent finishes*, in the settings menu). A click takes you to the agent, and a palette command goes to the next one that waits. dummyPlane also takes you there by itself, one agent at a time and oldest first, never while you are typing (or for 10 s after the last key while a field holds a draft you have not sent), or in the middle of a dialog or a menu, and not while you answer another agent (switch it off in the settings menu: *Go to agents that ask*).
- **A parent handles what its sub-agents ask, within limits.** An agent acts in the sub-agents it opened with `spawn_agent` (a Manager in its team, a Fleet also in its Manager; one further down is its own parent's), so a team is not stuck waiting for you on what its parent can judge. `run_command` types one of Claude Code's commands into an idle sub-agent at its prompt: `/compact` (with what to keep) or `/clear` (its conversation and its task are gone unless its parent sends the task again). Never other text, a shell command, a command that opens a panel (`/context`, `/cost`, which is now `/usage`), or one that changes your settings or account: `/model` is left out because Claude Code saves the model it sets as your default for new sessions. Nothing is typed while the sub-agent works, waits on a dialog, or holds text you have not sent. `answer_dialog` reads the dialog a sub-agent waits on, then picks one of its options, never by typing, and only if the same dialog is still on its screen. A parent may pick a permission request's *Yes* for this once or *No* (never *don't ask again* or *for this session*; a mission's Fleet or Manager says *Yes* only in a mission run in *Edit files and run commands*, and only *No* in *Ask* or *Edit files*), go on without a project's new MCP servers, or pick an answer to a question. A login, approving a repository's own MCP server, any other question a CLI asks before its session opens (an API key, a permission mode), and *Yes* to a prompt dummyPlane's guard raised (a recursive delete outside the agent's folder) stay yours. Every answer and every command shows a notice naming the parent, the sub-agent and the choice, and goes to the log.
- **Notifications that matter.** The bell on the right rail keeps three kinds of news, newest first: an agent that finished (with *Alert when an agent finishes* on; kept read when you were looking at it), a question an agent asks (read by itself once the agent no longer waits), and a Kanban board whose cards are all in its *Done* column, which also shows a popup with *Show* while the window is in focus (not the board of a running mission, whose end already tells it). Each says what it is, its text, when, and the project and workspace it was about. A click goes to that workspace, centres on the agent or board and marks it read. Filter by *All*, *This workspace*, *Questions*, *Finished* or *Boards*; *Mark all read* and *Clear* tidy it. Other popups are not kept. The last 200 survive a restart.
- **Remote Control.** Turn on Claude Code's Remote Control for one agent, a group or the whole team, and follow them from your phone.
- **Usage at a glance.** The title bar shows your Claude subscription's session and weekly limits. Click it for cost, durations, tokens per model, prompt cache state and an estimate of CO₂.
- **Tokens over 30 days.** From the usage detail, a chart of the tokens you used each day, and their share by agent and by model. dummyPlane reads the logs Claude Code already keeps on your disk, counters only, never the conversations; nothing is sent anywhere. Codex is not read yet.
- **Code Studio.** Fix a file by hand without leaving dummyPlane. Each project has a code workspace, an IDE tab beside its canvases, opened from the right rail or the function palette: the repository's tree, files in tabs with syntax colours in every palette, `Ctrl+S`, a Markdown preview, and *Insert* to put the file on a canvas. `Ctrl+P` finds a file, `App.tsx:42` included, and `Ctrl+G` goes to a line. If an agent wrote the file meanwhile, the save is refused and you choose: reload, or overwrite what you saw.
- **Notes.** Notes are Markdown files in `.dummyplane/notes/` of the project, so an agent can read them without dummyPlane. They open in a tab next to the workspace tabs. Link them with `[[name]]`, search them, see their backlinks and their graph, and attach notes to a group. A read-only tool lets agents list and read them.
- **Quick notes.** *Note* on a terminal's or a Fleet's right-click ring (or in its ⋯ menu, or the palette) opens a note right beside it, ready to type in. It follows its terminal when that is moved, minimised or arranged, and is saved with the canvas.
- **Pasting.** `Ctrl+V` in a terminal saves a clipboard image and types its path.
- **Install an agent from the app.** For an agent that is not found, *Install* shows the official command in full, then runs it in a visible terminal on the canvas that you can interrupt. Ollama models install the same way. Nothing is fetched or run in the background.
- **Loops.** Send an instruction again and again, with brakes. A loop fires every N minutes, when an agent goes idle, or when its screen shows a text; it must end after a number of turns (up to 1000) or a duration (up to 24 hours), whichever comes first. It never writes to an agent that waits for you, is stopped or is starting. An agent closed, stopped or exited (a finished one dummyPlane stopped among them) is no target until it runs again, and a loop with none left stops and says no agent is left. A watched text that shows while its agent is at work, waits for you or cannot take input fires once the agent is free again, if the text is still on screen by then: once per appearance, never typed into the agent's work. Its targets are agents only: a plain shell terminal is never offered nor typed into, and a loop saved when it could target one says it has no agent and stays off. Each project has its own loops (up to 30): its Loops window lists and turns on only those, aimed at its own agents, and a loop keeps running while you look at another project. A pill at the bottom left counts the running loops of every project and their turns, with *Stop all* one click away for all of them, and every loop is off again after a restart. Three templates (progress report, relaunch, watch the tests) fill the form but start nothing.
- **Several Claude accounts.** A profile is only a name and a folder (Claude Code's `CLAUDE_CONFIG_DIR`). *Claude accounts…* in the function palette lists them: *Use* makes one the account of the next Claude agents, *Log in* opens a visible terminal to sign in, *Remove* forgets a profile and leaves its folder alone. A terminal keeps the account it started with, and shows it in its header. dummyPlane never reads, copies or stores a credential.
- **A git window.** Click the branch chip at the bottom left: the files changed in the project, their diff, a tick per file to commit exactly those, the history, a *Switch branch* menu on the branch's name in its header (existing local branches only; git refuses a switch that would overwrite your changes), and a *Push* button that names its target (`Push 3 → origin/main`). Discarding a file asks first and says what is lost; this button is the only route to a push inside dummyPlane's own tools: it goes only when you click, and it never forces. An agent running commands in a terminal is limited only by its permissions.
- **Overnight run.** Say how long you will be away: the fleet plans, a team works through a queue of tickets, and a morning report waits for you. See [Overnight run](fleet.md#overnight-run), and read its warnings first.
- **Projects and workspaces.** One project per folder, several workspace tabs per project; drag the tabs to reorder them (or `Alt+←` / `Alt+→` on a tab, or its menu), and the order is remembered. dummyPlane saves everything locally and, after a restart, offers to resume your Claude conversations.
- **Keyboard first.** Use the palettes, jump between terminals, or right-press anywhere for the radial menu. Every ring ends with *More*, which opens a second arc, and every ring has *Arrange*, which arranges at once in the layout saved under *Settings → Arrange layout* (the whole canvas from the canvas's ring, a group from its ring or from a terminal's or Fleet's *More*, the selection from the selection's ring). To pick another layout (*Grid*, *One row*, *One column*, *2*, *3* or *4 columns*, *Tree*), use the palette's *Arrange as…* or *Arrange the group as…*, which open a list in the middle of the canvas: Up and Down move, Enter or a click arranges, Escape closes. Picked for the whole canvas, a layout is also kept as the *Arrange layout* setting; picked for a group or a selection, it is used once. A group's menu has the same layouts under *Arrange*. The canvas ring's *Other agent* and the palette's *Other agent…* open a list of the same kind, of the agents found on this machine (the Shell is the ring's own): it hangs from the point you pressed, and Up and Down move, Enter or a click starts the agent there, Escape closes. An agent that is not installed is not listed; *Install* stays in the ＋ menu and the palette. The terminals take the palette's colours.

## A tour

**Brief the whole team.** Type `@frontend` and the question. The three agents of the group work at the same time, then come back *ready*.

**Send a command to every agent.** After the targets, `/` lists Claude Code's commands; `/context` then runs in each session of the group.

**Review a change and send it back.** Jump to an agent with `Ctrl+P`, open *Review changes* from `Ctrl+Shift+P`, then click a line to leave a remark. *Send to Batman* sends every remark in one message.

**Check your usage.** The meter in the title bar opens the detail: subscription limits and when they reset, cost and durations of dummyPlane's sessions, tokens per model, and each session's prompt cache.

## Supported agents

dummyPlane launches the agents you already have installed: an agent counts as available when its command is on your `PATH`.

| Agent | Command | State detection |
|---|---|---|
| Claude Code | `claude` | Hooks (precise: running, waiting for you, done) |
| Codex | `codex` | Output, plus its `notify` hook at the end of each turn; it gets dummyPlane's tools through a profile in `$CODEX_HOME`, removed when its terminal closes |
| Gemini CLI | `gemini` | Output |
| Qwen Code | `qwen` | Output |
| Cursor | `cursor-agent` | Output |
| OpenCode | `opencode` | Output |
| Aider | `aider` | Output |
| Ollama | `ollama run <model>` (or Aider on an Ollama model) | Output |
| Shell | PowerShell, cmd, pwsh or Git Bash | Output |
| Custom | any command you type | Output |

Remote Control, missions, the model router, the step timeline and the usage meter rely on Claude Code; the canvas, groups, mentions and review work with every agent.

## Keyboard

| Shortcut | Action |
|---|---|
| `Ctrl+P` | Go to a terminal, group, fleet, workspace or project |
| `Ctrl+Shift+P` | Function palette (every command of the app) |
| `Ctrl+Tab` / `Ctrl+Shift+Tab` | Next / previous terminal, in reading order (those of a folded group are skipped: Go to, `Ctrl+P` and the Agents panel unfold it) |
| `Shift+Esc` | Give the keyboard back to the canvas |
| `Ctrl+Shift+A` | Overview |
| Right-press | Radial menu: click an item, or hold, drag and release |
| `@` · `/` · `↑` | In the instruction box: target · command · history |
| `Shift+Enter` | New line, in the instruction box and in terminals |
| `Ctrl+Shift+G` | Ungroup the selection on the canvas (never from inside a terminal or a field) |
| `Ctrl+V` | In a terminal: paste; a clipboard image is saved and its path pasted |
| `Alt+←` / `Alt+→` | Move the focused workspace tab left or right |
| `Ctrl+S` · `Ctrl+G` · `Ctrl+P` | In Code Studio: save the file · go to a line · find a file |

Excalidraw's own keys (`V`, `H`, `R`, `O`, `A`, `T`, `P`, `E`, `Ctrl+Z`…) work whenever the canvas has the keyboard. In a terminal, `Ctrl+C` copies when text is selected and interrupts otherwise.

## Single instance

dummyPlane runs once: launching it a second time brings the open window back (and restores it if it was minimized) instead of starting another copy, which would fight the first over the database and the log.

## How it works

- **Desktop shell:** [Tauri 2](https://v2.tauri.app/). The Rust side owns the PTYs ([portable-pty](https://crates.io/crates/portable-pty)), git, the SQLite database and a small HTTP server.
- **Interface:** React 19, TypeScript, Zustand and Tailwind CSS 4, with [Excalidraw](https://github.com/excalidraw/excalidraw) for the canvas and [xterm.js](https://xtermjs.org/) for the terminals.
- **Agent states and tools:** the HTTP server listens on `127.0.0.1` only, on a random port.
  - Each Claude Code session gets an extra `--settings` file. It declares hooks that report the session's state, a status line that reports usage, and an MCP server for the mission tools.
  - The same file has a `PreToolUse` hook that reads the Bash and PowerShell commands of every Claude session before they run, for deletes: an `rm`, `Remove-Item` or `rd` of a path Bash made drive-relative (`D:\temp\x` unquoted reaches `rm` as `D:tempx`), or a recursive one of a drive's root or of your home folder, is denied, and the agent is told to write the path with forward slashes. A recursive delete outside the folder the agent was started in (its worktree) asks you first, in its terminal, with what it removes and whether that exists; inside its own folder (`node_modules`, build output) or in the system temp folder (`%TEMP%`, Claude's scratchpad) nothing is asked, but `D:\temp` is not that folder. In a session that cannot ask (permission mode `dontAsk`, as in an overnight run) the same delete is refused, and the agent is told to delete the specific files it made by name or to ask you, not to try again. These are text rules read from the command as written, not a sandbox: `find -delete`, `xargs rm`, `cmd /c rd`, PowerShell's `Remove-Item -Recurse` and the variables the command set itself (`$HOME`, `%TEMP%`, `d=build`) are read, and a target that cannot be worked out asks; a delete done by a script on disk (`bash run.sh`, `powershell -File`) or by a program (`git clean -fdx`, `rsync --delete`, `python -c`) is not seen, and Codex and Gemini sessions have no such hook (the night dialog and the ＋ menu say so; see [fleet.md](fleet.md)).
  - Every request needs a token drawn when the app starts, or a token for the session itself.
  - dummyPlane reads your own `~/.claude/settings.json` but never modifies it, and your own status line keeps showing in the terminals.
- **Storage:** projects, canvases, terminal scrollback and settings live in a local SQLite database under `%APPDATA%\dev.dummyplane.app\`. Notes are plain files in the project's `.dummyplane/notes/`; team presets are a setting of the database.
- **Token counts:** read-only and bounded. dummyPlane scans the `.jsonl` logs under Claude Code's folder (`~/.claude/projects`, or `$CLAUDE_CONFIG_DIR`) for the last 30 days, keeps dates, models and counters, and drops the rest unread.

See [SECURITY.md](../SECURITY.md) for the security model in more detail.
